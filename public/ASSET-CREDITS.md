# Open-access image credits

The website stores local, web-sized derivatives of the following public-domain images. They are used as contextual illustration, not as evidence for the site’s chronology.

## Virgil Solis

- **Deucalion and Pyrrha**, from a 1563 illustrated edition of Ovid’s *Metamorphoses*. Public domain / Public Domain Mark. Source: [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Virgil_Solis_-_Deucalion_Pyrrha.jpg). Local derivative: `public/assets/engravings/book-01-deucalion.web.jpg`.

## The Metropolitan Museum of Art

- **Methamorphoseos vulgare**, Ovid, Venice, March 7, 1501. The Met marks object 354958 and its images as public domain under its Open Access program. Source: [The Met collection record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/354958). Local derivatives:
  - `public/assets/engravings/book-04-venus-mars.web.jpg`
  - `public/assets/engravings/met-plate-02.web.jpg`
  - `public/assets/engravings/met-plate-03.web.jpg`
  - `public/assets/engravings/met-plate-04.web.jpg`
  - `public/assets/engravings/met-plate-05.web.jpg`
  - `public/assets/engravings/met-plate-06.web.jpg`
  - `public/assets/engravings/met-folio/folio-01.jpg` through `folio-17.jpg`

The `met-folio` set uses The Met’s Open Access `web-large` derivatives exposed by the collection record’s `primaryImage` and `additionalImages` fields. The local files are used as a coherent visual register for book plates and clipped character medallions. No claim is made that every decorative crop is a literal portrait of the named figure or that every folio corresponds to the currently selected episode; exact episode-specific plates are identified explicitly in their captions.

## Reuse

The source institutions mark the images above as public domain. Preserve this file and the source links when redistributing the website so the visual provenance remains inspectable. Reusers outside the United States should review their jurisdiction’s treatment of faithful reproductions of public-domain two-dimensional works.
